## 1. Purpose

This example describes how personnel should seek help and use the site's formal
work-control system when they observe an abnormal condition or are planning
maintenance. It is intended to help personnel find the relevant controlled
procedure and responsible authority. It does not provide equipment-specific
operating limits, isolation points, valve line-ups, or a substitute for a job
hazard analysis.

## 2. Immediate response to an unsafe or abnormal condition

When a person observes a leak, unusual noise or vibration, unexpected movement,
an alarm, an unusual odor, smoke, or another potentially unsafe condition:

1. Do not approach, touch, adjust, open, or attempt to diagnose equipment if the
   area or condition may be unsafe.
2. Move to a safe location and warn nearby personnel when it is safe to do so.
3. Notify the control room or the designated site emergency contact promptly;
   activate the site's alarm or emergency notification method when required by
   the site emergency plan.
4. Follow the current site emergency and abnormal-situation instructions. Do
   not delay notification while collecting more information.
5. From a safe, authorized location, report the asset tag (if known), location,
   time, observed indication, alarm text, and any change in condition. Do not
   report a suspected cause as a confirmed diagnosis.
6. Keep clear until the responsible site authority has assessed the situation
   and given direction under the applicable site process.

If there is immediate danger, personnel should follow the facility's emergency
plan and evacuation or shelter instructions. This training example does not
specify emergency numbers, muster points, or alarm codes.

## 3. Maintenance work authorization

Before maintenance begins, the responsible site roles use the current work
management process to define the task, equipment boundary, hazards, required
precautions, permit category, and competent personnel. The job risk assessment
and permit must address the actual task and conditions at the time of work.

Work must not start until required reviews and authorizations are complete,
personnel understand the approved scope and controls, and the worksite is
released according to site rules. If the scope, process condition, or worksite
changes, stop and have the responsible authority reassess the work before
resuming.

The RAG demo does not establish who may issue or accept a permit at a real site.
Consult the facility's permit-to-work procedure and assigned roles.

## 4. Hazardous energy and equipment isolation

Where a task requires hazardous-energy control, authorized and competent site
personnel must identify all relevant energy sources, determine the isolation
boundary using current approved documents and the site energy-control process,
apply the required controls, and verify the safe condition using the approved
method before work is released. Personal locks, tags, group isolation, testing,
and restoration are governed by the facility's controlled procedure and the
specific job plan.

This document does not identify isolation valves or electrical disconnects for
P4711, T4750, or any other asset. A tag on a synthetic P&ID is not an approved
isolation plan. A user asking for an asset-specific isolation sequence must be
told that the available demo documents do not establish one and directed to the
current controlled P&ID, equipment-specific procedure, permit, and authorized
isolation authority.

## 5. Permits and task-specific precautions

The work planner and permit issuer determine which controls apply to the job.
Examples may include hot-work controls, confined-space entry controls, work at
height, excavation, electrical work, line breaking, or lifting operations.
This list is illustrative, not a determination that a particular permit is
required or sufficient. Follow the current site permit matrix and task risk
assessment.

Required protective equipment and monitoring depend on the hazards and the
facility's assessment. This example does not prescribe PPE, gas-test limits, or
entry criteria.

## 6. Interlocks, alarms, and protective devices

Alarms and protective devices must be managed under approved operating and
maintenance procedures. Personnel must not defeat, bypass, inhibit, bridge,
or otherwise impair an interlock or protective function without the formal
site-approved authorization and controls applicable to the facility. A RAG
answer is not such authorization and must not provide instructions for
defeating a safeguard.

For an alarm or suspected protective-device fault, follow the relevant
controlled alarm response or maintenance procedure and notify the responsible
operations authority. The synthetic demo material does not define real alarm
setpoints or bypass authorization.

## 7. Stop-work and escalation

Any person may raise a safety concern and request work to stop under the
facility's stop-work process. Stop and contact the responsible supervisor,
control room, or HSE authority if the approved scope is unclear, a required
control is unavailable, conditions differ from the assessment, or a person
believes the work may be unsafe. Resume only after the responsible site roles
have resolved the concern and reauthorized work as required by site procedure.

## 8. Records and handover

Record and hand over safety-relevant observations through the facility's
approved reporting system. Include factual observations, time, asset/location,
alarms, actions taken under approved instructions, notifications made, and
outstanding hazards. Do not substitute an informal note or an AI-generated
answer for required permits, logs, incident reports, or shift handover records.

## 9. Limits of this training example

The Area 47 tags and process details elsewhere in this corpus are fictional.
This document intentionally contains no real site emergency contact details,
asset-specific isolation instructions, operating limits, or regulatory claims.
For real work, retrieve the current controlled site document and confirm it
with the responsible qualified personnel.
